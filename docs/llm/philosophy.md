# Engineering Philosophy

> Audience: LLM collaborators
> Human counterpart: [Engineering Philosophy](../human/philosophy.md)
> Role: compact foundational commitments and ordering principles

The objective of Machines is not to produce code quickly.

The objective is to build a body of knowledge, tools, and systems that can be
understood, corrected, and extended across many decades.

## Foundational commitments

The following commitments govern the design of the project, its courses, its
tools, and the institutions built around them:

- domain truth comes before production;
- educational understanding comes before visual spectacle;
- concepts come before technologies;
- internal tools are first-class products;
- project-critical formats and data remain under project control rather than
  vendor control;
- consequential decisions preserve their reasoning and evidence;
- abstractions are discovered through real courses rather than speculation;
- replaceable parts remain separated from durable foundations;
- a solution is not accepted merely because it works;
- complexity is not built merely because time is available: every investment
  in depth must serve a purpose.

These are ordering principles, not excuses for indefinite analysis. Production,
visual quality, technology, and complexity are valuable when they serve the
truth of the Machine, the learner's understanding, and the durable capabilities
of Machines.

## Day-to-day priorities

Within those commitments, the project prioritizes:

- truth before convenience;
- understanding before abstraction;
- readability before compression;
- cohesion before accidental reuse;
- explicit boundaries before implicit coupling;
- durable semantics before temporary technology;
- evidence before confidence;
- simple explanations before unnecessary complexity;
- replaceability where knowledge is still incomplete;
- depth where it materially improves the learner's understanding.

Every component should have a clear responsibility. Every consequential
decision should preserve its reasoning. A simpler solution should be preferred
when it produces the same verified result and preserves the same long-term
potential.

Code will be read, questioned, migrated, and revised many more times than it is
first written. Documentation, tests, models, research evidence, and migration
paths are therefore part of the engineering work rather than accessories to it.

Depth is not the accumulation of features. It is the removal of unexamined
assumptions until the project can explain why a system exists, which records or
decisions it governs, how it can fail, and how its result can be verified.

The full decision framework is defined in the
[long-horizon engineering doctrine](engineering-doctrine.md).
