# Vision

> Audience: LLM collaborators
> Human counterpart: [Vision](../human/vision.md)
> Role: mission, learner promise, experience character, and product boundaries

## Mission

Help people understand the machines that shape the modern world through
interactive construction experiences.

The project begins from a conviction: a powerful way to understand a machine is
to construct it step by step. Each added Component represents a new idea made
intelligible. The learner gradually reaches a complete system and can explain
how its parts cooperate to perform a function.

Machines does not primarily teach isolated theory. It develops intuition that
can survive beyond the course interface.

## The problem

People live among extraordinary machines: phones, vehicles, computers,
satellites, electric motors, transformers, networks, processors, biological
systems, and modern artificial intelligence.

Most remain black boxes to most people.

Common educational resources often:

- explain concepts before giving the learner a coherent system to reason about;
- divide knowledge by academic subject rather than by real systems;
- depend heavily on passive text and video;
- present too much information before it becomes useful;
- require the learner to imagine complex structures without sufficient spatial
  support;
- reward terminology recall without establishing causal relationships.

The result can be familiarity without a strong mental model. A learner may know
names or formulas while remaining unable to explain how the system's parts
produce its behavior.

## The proposed learning form

The central product act is simple:

> Construct to understand.

Each Course guides the learner from a deliberately bounded starting point to a
complete primary Machine Variant. During construction, the learner discovers:

- which Components exist;
- which function each Component performs;
- how Components constrain, enable, transform, or communicate with one another;
- why the design uses those relationships;
- how local functions cooperate to produce the complete result.

Learning becomes an active process of observing, predicting, placing,
connecting, explaining, checking, and revising.

## Why construction

Machines are systems. Systems become intelligible when their parts and
relationships can be encountered in a meaningful order.

Construction supports that order:

- each Component can answer a visible question;
- each action can expose a Relation;
- knowledge can appear when the learner needs it;
- complexity can grow gradually;
- the partially constructed state can preserve causal context;
- the final system can be understood as accumulated relationships rather than
  an unexplained whole.

The objective is not merely for the learner to recognize names. The stronger
test is whether the learner can explain how the machine works when the course
is no longer in front of them, reason about a changed configuration, and apply
important concepts elsewhere.

## Definition of a machine

Within the product vision, a machine is a bounded system whose Components
cooperate to perform a function.

It can be:

- mechanical;
- electrical or electronic;
- biological;
- computational or logical;
- digital;
- hybrid.

Scale does not determine eligibility. A lock, cell, motor, processor, satellite,
or artificial-intelligence system may each be studied as a Machine when its
boundary, Components, Relations, and function can be made educationally
coherent.

The [conceptual model](concepts.md) provides the rigorous definition and
invariants.

## Definition of a course

A Course is the complete deliberate path for constructing and understanding
one primary Machine Variant.

During a Course, the learner:

- encounters Components in a designed sequence;
- assembles or connects them through meaningful actions;
- understands the function of each included part;
- develops a model of their important relationships;
- demonstrates reasoning at defined points;
- finishes with an integrated explanation of the system.

The result is not only an assembled representation. It is a clearer mental
model of how the real machine works within the declared scope.

## Definition of a lesson

A Lesson is one coherent advance in construction and understanding. It has one
primary learning objective and introduces only the complexity needed to reach
that objective.

At the end of a Lesson, the learner should have changed the Assembly State and
be able to explain the educational meaning of that change.

## Learning principles

### Gradual construction

Complexity appears through deliberate stages. The Course may preview the final
purpose or silhouette when that creates useful orientation, but it should not
present the complete internal explanation before the learner has a structure
for understanding it.

### Understanding before memorization

Explanations develop intuition, causal reasoning, and system relationships.
Technical language is introduced when it improves precision after meaning is
available.

### Curiosity before disclosure

Each new Component should answer a question while creating a reason to inspect
the next part. The sequence should preserve meaningful uncertainty without
manufacturing confusion.

### Every included element has a purpose

If a Component, animation, camera movement, explanation, check, or interaction
appears, its educational purpose should be identifiable and testable.

### Active learning

The learner participates through observation, choice, assembly, prediction,
explanation, and correction. Activity is not sufficient by itself; each action
must contribute to reasoning.

### Rigor without unnecessary complexity

The Course should be precise about the selected Machine Variant and honest
about simplification. Detail that does not advance the target understanding can
be omitted. Simplification must not create a false causal relationship.

### Contextual help

Help should respond to the exact Course, Lesson, Assembly State, visible
Components, prior presentation, and current difficulty. The tutor should feel
like an inhabitant of the learning experience rather than a generic chat layer.

### Continuity

The learner should be able to leave and resume without losing the semantic
state of the construction or the educational context required to continue.

## Product boundaries

Machines is not primarily intended to:

- replace professional engineering, modeling, or simulation systems;
- reproduce every physical phenomenon at maximum fidelity;
- teach through long passive video lectures;
- add complexity because it is technically possible;
- sacrifice clarity for realism;
- use interaction as decoration;
- treat course completion as sufficient evidence of understanding;
- become a generic content library with a three-dimensional viewer;
- place a generic chatbot around conventional lessons.

Scoped simulation, video, rich rendering, or conversation can be used when they
serve an explicit educational purpose. The boundary is about product authority,
not categorical prohibition of a medium.

## Desired experience

The learner should experience a continuing sense of discovery. Each Component
should make the Machine more intelligible while opening the next useful
question.

The experience should feel:

- clear without feeling simplistic;
- rigorous without becoming needlessly academic;
- visually excellent without becoming spectacle;
- active without becoming game-like noise;
- guided without removing agency;
- coherent enough that the complete Machine feels earned.

At the end of a meaningful session, the learner should be able to think:

> I understand how this works more clearly than I did before.

At the end of a Course, the real machine should feel less mysterious. The
learner should see an organized system of purposeful Components and Relations.

## Quality ceiling

The long-term ambition is not merely to make educational content more
interactive. It is to create a native medium for understanding machines:

- a semantic language born from Machines;
- authoring tools that reason about machine and learning structure;
- representations designed for explanation;
- a runtime organized around assembly and understanding;
- pedagogical intelligence with a distinctive, evaluated identity;
- a Course Factory that becomes more capable through accumulated evidence.

This ambition does not justify building every system before it is understood.
The reference course is the first instrument for discovering which parts of the
vision need native expression and what “excellent” means in observable terms.

## Current vision-level decision

The immediate unresolved choice is the first reference Machine and its exact
variant. It should be small enough to study completely and rich enough to test
the central promise: construction that produces real understanding rather than
mere manipulation.
