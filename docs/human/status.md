# Current Project Position

> Detailed counterpart: [LLM project state](../llm/status.md)

## Where Machines is now

Machines is in foundational planning and pre-reference-course selection.

The mission, conceptual direction, 15-stage course pipeline, native-tool
ambition, Course Factory hypothesis, architecture boundaries, and long-term
research programs have been documented. They are planning contracts, not claims
that the systems already exist.

## What is established

- People learn a machine by constructing it piece by piece and understanding
  the relationships that make it work.
- Each course has exactly one primary machine variant and a deliberate boundary.
- Domain truth precedes simplification and asset production.
- The first complete low-fidelity course is a research instrument, not a throwaway
  demo.
- Native tools must share Machines semantics rather than behave as disconnected
  generic applications.
- External technology can support research, but its role and replacement path
  must be explicit.
- The production foundation will be a Machines-native engine, not Unity or
  another generic game engine; its exact internal architecture remains open to
  evidence from Course work.
- The creator owns Machine, Course, and learner-experience authorship; the LLM
  owns enabling engineering and operations by default and escalates only across
  declared experience, policy, risk, cost, or irreversibility boundaries.

## Active decision

Select the first reference course and then select its exact machine variant.

The next decision packet should compare a small set of candidate machines using:

- strength of natural curiosity;
- clarity of function;
- educational value of assembly;
- observable component relationships;
- manageable but nontrivial scope;
- quality and availability of evidence;
- representation feasibility;
- ability to exercise the minimum vertical engine;
- concepts likely to transfer to later courses.

## Near decision sequence

1. Select the reference machine category.
2. Select and bound one exact variant.
3. Build Generation 0: a complete low-fidelity course.
4. Identify the minimum native vertical system required to execute it.
5. Implement that vertical slice and measure where the abstractions fail.
6. Revise, then test the abstractions against a deliberately different course.

## What is intentionally not decided

- the final internal engine architecture and lower-level technology boundary;
- the final authoring interface;
- the final rendering implementation;
- the final pedagogical model architecture;
- the full catalog;
- the permanent boundary of every external dependency.

Those decisions need evidence from real course work.
