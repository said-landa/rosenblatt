# Current Project Position

> Detailed counterpart: [LLM project state](../llm/status.md)

## Where Machines is now

Machines has selected its first reference Course and built its first executable
Generation 0 vertical slice.

The Course teaches the normal operation of a Master Lock W1 four-pin
pin-tumbler cylinder. Its authored source, semantic validator/compiler,
deterministic assembly runtime, resumable progress state, automated tests, and
native macOS low-fidelity preview now exist. This proves an executable path, not
Course quality or a final architecture.

## What is established

- People learn a machine by constructing it piece by piece and understanding
  the relationships that make it work.
- Each course has exactly one primary machine variant and a deliberate boundary.
- Domain truth precedes simplification and asset production.
- The first complete low-fidelity course is a research instrument, not a throwaway
  demo.
- Native tools must share Machines semantics rather than behave as disconnected
  generic applications.
- External technology can support research, but its role and replacement path
  must be explicit.
- The production foundation will be a Machines-native engine, not Unity or
  another generic game engine; its exact internal architecture remains open to
  evidence from Course work.
- The creator owns Machine, Course, and learner-experience authorship; the LLM
  owns enabling engineering and operations by default and escalates only across
  declared experience, policy, risk, cost, or irreversibility boundaries.
- The first primary Machine Variant is the W1 four-pin cylinder, bounded to its
  shell, plug, pin stacks, springs, compatible key, and shear-line function.
- Generation 0 compiles ten assembly and reasoning steps from semantic JSON and
  runs them without a generic game engine or external package.
- The native preview has been visually traversed through its normal, mistaken,
  hint, completion, and restart paths; this is implementation evidence, not
  creator acceptance or learner validation.

## Active decision

Decide whether Generation 0 tells the right complete functional story before
promoting its provisional tool contracts.

The immediate evidence needed is:

- creator review of the ten-step learning and interaction path;
- independent review of W1 applicability and mechanical accuracy;
- end-to-end visual and interaction inspection;
- correction of defects at the pipeline stage that caused them.

## Near decision sequence

1. Inspect and correct Generation 0.
2. Obtain domain and creator acceptance of its bounded functional story.
3. Run low-fidelity prototype sessions and record findings.
4. Revise the Course and vertical implementation from that evidence.
5. Promote only contracts that survive the complete Course.
6. Test promoted contracts against a deliberately different Course.

## What is intentionally not decided

- the final internal engine architecture and lower-level technology boundary;
- the final authoring interface;
- the final rendering implementation;
- the final pedagogical model architecture;
- the full catalog;
- the permanent boundary of every external dependency.

Those decisions need evidence from real course work.

The current schematic is approximate, the content has not been independently
domain-reviewed, the experience has not been creator-accepted or piloted, and no
release is implied.
