# Rosenblatt

Rosenblatt is a 3D game about building a civilization and discovering how human technology emerges from the natural world. Starting with no manufactured possessions, players learn to turn stone, wood, plant fibers, and minerals into clothing, shelter, tools, machines, and eventually the technologies of modern life.

Play alone or build alongside friends. The journey connects the first useful tool to a radio, a computer, and a satellite through the materials, processes, and inventions that make each achievement possible.

The game is in early development. This README describes its intended experience; the gameplay systems described here are planned.

## From raw materials to civilization

Players begin with immediate needs: finding useful materials, making their first clothing and tools, and building a place to live. Each discovery expands what they can do and helps their settlement grow.

As the civilization develops, players establish the means to extract resources, process materials, manufacture components, and assemble increasingly capable technology. Advanced components come from production chains the players have helped create. A finished machine carries the story of the materials and earlier inventions behind it.

Human technological history guides the broad progression, from early tools and shelters through metallurgy, mechanical power, electricity, electronics, computing, and space technology. Historical time is compressed into a playable journey, with attention to the dependencies between inventions and the different paths through which technologies developed.

## Progress that changes how you play

Each major invention should create a meaningful new capability or make familiar work easier. Better tools, machines, and the growing settlement allow players to move on to new challenges as established processes become more efficient.

Gathering and crafting should make the origins of technology understandable without demanding endless repetition. Players participate in the important transformations, see their consequences, and use what they learn to build something more ambitious.

The civilization is also a community. Planned NPC inhabitants bring activity and character to the settlement, alongside the player and any friends who join them.

## Assembly as an artistic experience

Making things is central to Rosenblatt. Carefully staged animations, expressive visual effects, sound, and music reveal how materials become components and how components work together as a machine.

These sequences celebrate human ingenuity while helping players understand what they are building. The reward continues beyond assembly: the completed invention works in the world and changes what the civilization can achieve.

## A believable, focused 3D world

Rosenblatt takes place in bounded, deliberately designed environments. Its visual direction combines believable materials, lighting, landscapes, and motion with practical rendering and simulation suited to lightweight hardware.

## Custom engine

Rosenblatt will use its own engine, Crocante Engine, developed around the needs of this game. Development has begun in Rust in [`crocante-engine/`](crocante-engine/).

The engine will grow alongside playable game experiences, with rendering, animation, assembly presentation, world interaction, and content tools developed as the game requires them. Existing libraries can provide foundations for individual systems.

Desktop, mobile, and consoles are intended publishing destinations. Platform support and release readiness will be established through implementation, testing, and each platform's publishing process.
